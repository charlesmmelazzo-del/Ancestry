Harvey Line — Generation 8

Mary Glendenning Hoggatt

1698–1780

Born in Edinburgh, Scotland in 1698. Crossed the Atlantic as a young woman, married a Quaker in colonial America, lived to eighty-two, and was buried at Springfield Friends Meeting in North Carolina.

Documented

David George Glendenning (fl. late 1600s – early 1700s) Margaret Hall Glendenning (fl. late 1600s) │ ▼ Mary Glendenning Hoggatt (1698–1780) ⚭ Philip Hoggett

Mary Glendenning was born on the 8th of December, 1698, in Edinburgh, Scotland, to David George Glendenning and Margaret Hall. She grew up with at least four siblings — Agnes, Walter, Isabell, and John. Sometime in the second decade of the eighteenth century she crossed the Atlantic, settling in colonial America.

By 1720 she had married Philip Hoggatt (the surname was variously spelled Hoggett or Hockett in the colonial records). Together they raised at least seven children — David, Jean (later Bailey), William (whose descendants used the Hockett spelling), John, Anthony P. Hoggatt, Sr. (Mike's gen-7 ancestor), Joseph, and Jane. The family was Quaker — members of the Society of Friends — and lived in the Pennsylvania, Maryland, and later Carolina Friends communities.

Mary lived to be eighty-two. She died on the 17th of November, 1780, in Deep River, Guilford County, North Carolina, during the American Revolution. She was buried at Springfield Friends Meeting — a Guilford County Quaker burial ground established just seven years earlier in 1773 with the Quaker migration from Pennsylvania and Virginia into the Carolina Piedmont.

This Scottish-born emigrant is almost certainly the source of the muddled "Highland Scotland" family memory that earlier records had mistakenly attached to the Carmichael/McMichael side. The actual Scottish-born ancestor in the tree was Mary Glendenning of Edinburgh — on the Hoggatt-Harvey side, not the McMichael side. The family memory was real; only the attribution was wrong.
