Quesenberry Line — Generation 4

Rebecca Jane Smyth Quesenberry Travis

1844–1920

Widowed at nineteen by the Civil War. Buried two husbands and raised at least seven children. Lived to see her grandson Floyd come of age.

Documented

Edward McDonald Smyth (fl. early 1800s) Minerva Parsons Smyth (fl. early 1800s) │ ▼ Rebecca Jane Smyth Quesenberry Travis (1844–1920) ⚭ Pvt. Alexander Henry Quesenberry, Sr. │ ▼ Alexander Henry Quesenberry, Jr. (1863–1937) Mary E. Quesenberry Graham (fl. mid-late 1800s)

Rebecca Jane Smyth was born on the 16th of November, 1844, in Lee County, Virginia — far up in the Cumberland Gap country where Virginia, Kentucky, and Tennessee come together. Her father was Edward McDonald Smyth (sometimes spelled Smythe), her mother Minerva Parsons. She grew up with at least eight siblings — Henry, Emily, Martha, William, Minerva, Mary Frances, Robert, and James Tennessee.

Around 1862 she married Pvt. Alexander Henry Quesenberry, Sr. — perhaps just weeks before he marched off with his Confederate regiment. He was twenty; she was eighteen. The marriage produced at least two children: Mary E. Quesenberry (who would later marry into the Graham family) and Alexander Henry Quesenberry, Jr., born on the 29th of July 1863 — three months after his father was killed at the Battle of Chancellorsville. Rebecca was nineteen years old, a widow with two small children, by the end of that summer.

She did not stay widowed. After the war she married James T. Travis (also seen as James F. Travis) and bore him at least five more children — James Edward, Robert Lee, Nannie J., Nancy Jane (later Garrett), Frances M. — and possibly Sarah, Mattie, and Pelitha as well. The blended Quesenberry-Travis household lived in Lee County, Virginia, for the rest of her long life.

She died in 1920, age 76, at Jonesville — the Lee County seat — the same year her great-grandson Lonnie Olin Quesenberry was born in Kingsport, Tennessee. The two never met, but both were born and raised in the same Cumberland Gap country, just on different sides of the state line.
