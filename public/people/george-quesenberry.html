Quesenberry Line — Generation 5

George Quesenberry

1748–1812

American Revolutionary soldier, frontier settler — he carried the Quesenberry name from the colonial Tidewater into the Blue Ridge.

⚔ American Revolutionary War — Montgomery County, Virginia Militia

George Quesenberry was born in 1748 in Orange County, Virginia, in the rich Piedmont west of the Tidewater settlements. He was a son of Aaron Quisenberry Sr. and Joyce Gayle Dudley — a third-generation American whose great-grandfather had crossed the Atlantic during the reign of King James I.

When the Revolution came, George served with the Montgomery County, Virginia Militia — one of the western militia units that defended the Virginia frontier during the war and that, on occasion, marched east to join the Continental Army or skirmish with Loyalists. The Montgomery County militia was active throughout the war, and Quesenberrys appear on its muster rolls.

After the Revolution, George followed the southwestern Virginia migration into the high country that would become Floyd County in 1831. He died there in 1812 — when the United States was at war with Britain a second time. His sons would clear and farm the same Greasy Creek and Indian Valley hollows that his great-great-grandsons would still be working a century later.
