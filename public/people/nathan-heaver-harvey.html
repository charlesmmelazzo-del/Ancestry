Harvey Line — Generation 6

Nathan Heaver Harvey

1759–1837

Pennsylvania Quaker, born in William Penn's colony at the eve of the Revolution. Migrated south to Orange County, NC, where he died at seventy-seven.

Documented

Nathan Heaver Harvey (1759–1837) ⚭ Agnes Harvey │ ▼ Anthony Harvey (1791–1846)

Nathan Heaver Harvey was born on the 21st of September, 1759, in Chester County, Pennsylvania — the heart of William Penn's Quaker colony, where his Quaker forebears had settled in the late seventeenth century. He came of age during the American Revolution; at sixteen, when the war began, he would have been of fighting age, but as a Quaker his religious tradition would have led him to refuse military service. (Pennsylvania Quakers played a complex role in the Revolution: many refused arms; some were disowned by their meetings for fighting; others quietly supported the cause through hospital work, farming for the army, or simply continuing their lives.)

He married Agnes Harvey (1756–1834) — note the shared surname, suggesting either a cousin marriage or the convention of Quaker women keeping their birth surname. Together they raised at least one son, Anthony Harvey (born 23 April 1791, Alamance County, NC). Their move from Chester County PA to Orange County NC was part of the great Quaker migration south down the Great Wagon Road in the late eighteenth century — a migration that brought many Quaker families from Pennsylvania into the new Quaker settlements of Piedmont North Carolina (most famously the New Garden community in what became Guilford County).

Nathan died on the 12th of March, 1837, in Orange County, NC, age 77. His descendants would carry the Harvey name through five more American generations to William Carl Harvey (1887–1966) — Ruth's father.
