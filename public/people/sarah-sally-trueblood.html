Harvey Line — Generation 4

Sarah Sally Trueblood Harvey

1812–before 1870

Born Guilford NC 21 September 1812. Widowed in 1846. Migrated west to Missouri where she died before 1870 — leaving four-year-old Isaac J. behind in North Carolina.

Documented

Isaac Trueblood (fl. late 1700s – early 1800s) Mary Outland Trueblood (fl. late 1700s – early 1800s) │ ▼ Sarah Sally Trueblood Harvey (1812–before 1870) ⚭ Anthony Harvey │ ▼ Isaac J. Harvey (1842–1912)

Sarah Sally Trueblood was born on the 21st of September, 1812, in Guilford County, North Carolina, to Isaac Trueblood and Mary Outland Trueblood. She grew up with at least eight siblings: Martha, Jonathan J., Isabella (later Bradshaw), Mary Outland (named for her mother), Rachel, Milicent, Jesse B., and Thomas. The Trueblood surname is a famous English Quaker name — the Trueblood Quakers were among the earliest settlers of Perquimans and Pasquotank Counties on North Carolina's Albemarle Sound, dating to the 1670s, before later branches migrated inland to the Piedmont counties.

She married Anthony Harvey (1791–1846), and at age 30 (in 1842) bore their son Isaac J. Harvey — likely the only surviving child. Anthony died in 1846 when Isaac was just four years old, leaving Sarah a young widow in her early thirties.

Sometime in the 1850s or 1860s Sarah made the dramatic decision to migrate west to Missouri — perhaps following an extended-family migration, perhaps remarrying. She died there before 1870, leaving her son Isaac behind in North Carolina, where he would grow up, marry three times, and father William Carl Harvey — Ruth's father.
