Harvey Line — Generation 8

Judith Deuce Osborne Harvey

1683–1770

Born in Staffordshire, England in 1683. Emigrated to William Penn's colony, married twice, bore at least ten children, lived to eighty-seven. The deepest English-born ancestor in this tree.

Documented

Samuel Deuce (fl. mid-late 1600s) Elizabeth Deuce (fl. mid-late 1600s) │ ▼ Judith Deuce Osborne Harvey (1683–1770) ⚭ William HARVEY (colonial)

Judith Deuce was born in 1683 in Bilston, Staffordshire, England — in what is now the Black Country of greater Wolverhampton, then a region of small ironworks, coal pits, and dissenting religious communities. Her father was Samuel Deuce; her mother Elizabeth Deuce (maiden name unrecovered). Bilston was a stronghold of early religious nonconformity — Quaker, Methodist, and Baptist congregations were all rooted there — and Judith almost certainly grew up in one of these dissenting families.

Sometime in the early 1700s — likely as a young woman or young wife — Judith joined the great migration of English Quakers and other nonconformists to William Penn's Pennsylvania. There she married first Peter Osborne, and with him bore at least five children: Elizabeth Ann (later Sheward), Charles, Peter, Suzanna, and Samuel Sr. After Peter Osborne's death she married, as her second husband, William Harvey, I — Mike's eighth-generation direct ancestor. With William she bore at least six more children: Hannah (later Way), William II, Isaac L. Harvey (born 1718 at Chadds Ford, Pennsylvania), James, Amos, and Anne (later Bayley).

Judith lived an extraordinarily long life — eighty-seven years across two marriages and at least eleven children. She died on the 1st of May, 1770, in Chester, Pennsylvania — still in the same Chester County Quaker community where her descendants would remain for another half-century before migrating south to Orange and Guilford Counties, North Carolina.

Judith is the deepest English-born ancestor in the Harvey line — pushing the documented Harvey ancestry back to the reign of Charles II and putting the family's origin firmly in the English Midlands.
