Mcmichael Line — Generation 4

Jesse C. McMichael

1820–1907

Father of William Franklin McMichael, grandfather of Geneva. His mother was a Pegram, his son's wife was a Pegram — the McMichael-Pegram intermarriage runs at least three generations deep in Deep River.

Documented

William Aydlott McMichael, Sr. (fl. late 1700s – early 1800s) Rhoda E. Pegram McMichael (fl. late 1700s – early 1800s) │ ▼ Jesse C. McMichael (1820–1907) ⚭ Anna Stephens McMichael │ ▼ William Franklin McMichael (1849–1941)

Jesse C. McMichael was born on the 27th of October, 1820, in Guilford County, North Carolina — the year of the Missouri Compromise. His father was William Aydlott McMichael, Sr.; his mother was Rhoda E. Pegram McMichael. The mother's maiden name is the most important detail in this entire branch: it reveals that the Pegram and McMichael families had been intermarrying in Guilford County since before Jesse was born.

Jesse was one of at least eleven siblings — Mary Ann (who married a Pegram), Joseph Frank, Sarah H. (later Highfill), Thomas A., Archibald McMichael, Nancy (who also married a Pegram), Obadiah, John Watson, Charity, and William Aydelott Jr. (Note: The brother Archibald McMichael almost certainly explains the muddled "Archibald Carmichael / Highland Scotland" memory that earlier family records mistakenly attached to this branch. The real Archibald was Jesse's brother — a McMichael born and raised in Guilford NC, not a Scottish patriarch.)

Jesse married Anna Stephens sometime before 1845. Together they raised nine known children: Sarah J. (later Highfill), George A., Rhoda Melissa (later Rayle), William Franklin McMichael — Geneva's father — John W., Washington C., Mary, Martha (later Parish), and Jesse A. The 1850 federal census places the household in Guilford County with George, Sarah, Rhoda, and William still small. The 1880 census places them at Bruce, Guilford County — a Deep River community — with Ann and three grown daughters still at home.

Jesse lived eighty-seven years. He died on the 9th of February, 1907 — having seen James Monroe inaugurated, the Civil War destroy the South he was born into, Reconstruction redraw his county, the Wright Brothers fly at Kitty Hawk, and Theodore Roosevelt elected to a full term as president. He was photographed at least once in his later years — a MyHeritage record-match preserves the personal photograph.

The double Pegram-McMichael intermarriage that defines Geneva's lineage now becomes visible: Geneva's father William Franklin McMichael had a Pegram grandmother (Rhoda Pegram McMichael) AND a Pegram mother-in-law (Sally Pegram, daughter of James Fletcher Pegram). The two families had effectively merged into a single Deep River extended kin-network across three generations.
