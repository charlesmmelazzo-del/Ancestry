Aaron Quisenberry Sr.

1725–1795

DAR Patriot Ancestor #A093307 — furnished beef to Washington's Continental Army from his Orange County, Virginia farm.

✓ Documented from public records

⚔ American Revolution — DAR Patriot Ancestor #A093307; furnished beef to the Continental Army

Aaron Quisenberry Sr. was born in 1725 in Caroline County, Virginia — when George I sat on the British throne and the colonies were still half a century away from independence. He married Joyce Gayle Dudley in King George County in 1741, when he was sixteen. Together they would have ten children over the next twenty-nine years.

When the American Revolution came, Aaron — by then a man in his fifties with grown sons of fighting age — supported the cause materially: he furnished beef from his Orange County farm to the Continental Army. This service is recognized by both the Daughters of the American Revolution and the National Society Sons of the American Revolution, which list him as a verified Patriot Ancestor. Any descendant of Aaron Quisenberry — and the Quesenberry family has many thousands of descendants — is therefore eligible for membership in the DAR or SAR through his line.

He died on the 11th of March, 1795, in St. Thomas Parish, Orange County, Virginia. His son George had already taken the family name into the Blue Ridge frontier.
