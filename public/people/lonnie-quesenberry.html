Quesenberry Line — Generation 1

Lonnie Olin Quesenberry

1920–2002

Born in the Tennessee hill country, came of age in the Norfolk shipyards on the eve of the Second World War, married Ruth Harvey in Greensboro, and lived out his life in High Point — three states in one American lifetime.

Documented

Floyd Harmon Quesenberry (1884–1970) Eva Elizabeth Mullins Quesenberry (1890–1983) │ ▼ Lonnie Olin Quesenberry (1920–2002) ⚭ Ruth Garland Harvey Quesenberry

Lonnie Olin Quesenberry was born on the 18th of March, 1920, in Kingsport, Sullivan County, Tennessee — in the far northeast corner of that state, where the Holston River runs out of southwestern Virginia and the Cumberland Gap is one ridge over. The Quesenberry family had been in the Floyd County, Virginia mountains for five generations; by Lonnie's birth they had drifted south and west, across the state line, into the new industrial town of Kingsport (which had been incorporated only three years before, in 1917, as a planned community around a Mead pulp-and-paper mill).

His father, Floyd Harmon Quesenberry, was thirty-five when Lonnie was born; his mother, Eva Elizabeth Mullins, was twenty-eight. Lonnie had at least one sibling — Billie R. Quesenberry — who appears alongside him in the 1940 federal census household, both still children at home. By 1935 the family had moved again, this time east to Western Branch, Norfolk, Virginia — five miles from the great Naval Operating Base, where the buildup to the Second World War would soon make work plentiful for any able-bodied young man with mechanical aptitude.

Lonnie turned twenty-one in March 1941, nine months before Pearl Harbor. On 16 July of that year he is recorded in a Norfolk newspaper. His WWII Draft Registration Card, since recovered, was filed at Bowers Hill, Norfolk, Virginia — the Naval shipyard country. Whether he served at sea or worked the yards through the war years is not yet known; the National Archives service record will tell the rest of that story.

By 1949 Lonnie was settled in Greensboro, North Carolina — newly married. On the 17th of July, 1948 — when the country was rebuilding itself from the war — Lonnie had married Ruth Garland Harvey in Greensboro. He was 28; she was 23, valedictorian of Colfax High School a few years before.

Together they raised three children — Phillip Olyn, Cathey Arlene, and Carol Jeanette — in High Point, the furniture-making capital of the South. He died on the 22nd of December, 2002, at the age of 82, in High Point Care Center, and was buried at Holly Hill Memorial Park in Thomasville. His wife of fifty-four years would lie beside him fourteen years later.
