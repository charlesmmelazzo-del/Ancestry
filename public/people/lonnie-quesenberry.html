Quesenberry Line — Generation 1

Lonnie Olin Quesenberry

1920–2002

Husband, father, and a quiet bridge between the Virginia mountains his family had farmed for two centuries and the new world of the Carolina Piedmont.

Documented

Floyd H. Quesenberry (fl. 1920s) Eva Quesenberry (fl. 1920s) │ ▼ Lonnie Olin Quesenberry (1920–2002) ⚭ Ruth Garland Harvey Quesenberry

Lonnie Olin Quesenberry was born on the 18th of March, 1920, into a country still shaking off the influenza pandemic and the weariness of a great war. He carried in his name an old American story: the Quesenberry surname, mountain-Virginian for nearly two centuries before his birth, and the middle name Olin — a soft, Scandinavian-sounding name that had drifted south with his people from the Blue Ridge into the rolling Piedmont of North Carolina.

His father, Floyd H. Quesenberry, almost certainly took his given name from the Virginia county where the Quesenberrys had been numerous since the eighteenth century. His mother Eva — a name that recurs through the Quesenberry family for at least four generations — raised Lonnie in the kind of close, intermarried Appalachian community where cousins and second cousins married each other and the same surnames repeated on every fence post and gravestone.

Lonnie came of age in the Great Depression. He turned twenty-one in March 1941, nine months before Pearl Harbor. Like most American men born in 1920, he would have registered for the WWII draft, though the specific record of his service is not yet located in the public files. What we know for certain is that on the 17th of July, 1948 — when the country was rebuilding itself from war and the postwar baby boom was beginning — Lonnie married Ruth Garland Harvey in Greensboro, North Carolina. He was 28; she was 23, and just back from her valedictory speech at Colfax High School a few years before.

Together they raised three children — Phillip Olyn, Cathey Arlene, and Carol Jeanette — in High Point, the furniture-making capital of the South. He died on the 22nd of December, 2002, at the age of 82, and was buried at Holly Hill Memorial Park in Thomasville. His wife of fifty-four years would lie beside him fourteen years later.
