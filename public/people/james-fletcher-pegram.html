James Fletcher Pegram

1831–1915

Father of Sally Pegram, grandfather of Geneva. Married 1853 in Forsyth County, lived eighty-four years through the Civil War, Reconstruction, and the start of the Great War.

Documented

Jesse Pegram (fl. early 1800s) Lucretia Juanita Fuller Pegram (fl. early 1800s) │ ▼ James Fletcher Pegram (1831–1915) ⚭ Annie Elizabeth Gates Pegram │ ▼ Sally Pegram McMichael (1860–?)

James Fletcher Pegram was born on the 4th of May, 1831, in Rockingham County, North Carolina — born to Jesse Pegram and Lucretia Juanita Fuller Pegram (sometimes spelled Loucretia in his death certificate). The Pegrams were an established Piedmont North Carolina family with English roots — the surname was sometimes recorded as Pilgrim in colonial-era documents.

On the 10th of March, 1853, in Forsyth County, North Carolina, James (then 21) married Annie Elizabeth Gates, who was actually five years his senior. Together they raised at least five children in Deep River Township, Guilford County — William Henry, Mary D., John C., Sally (Geneva's mother), and Nancy E. The 1900 and 1910 federal censuses both place him in Deep River with Annie and three of their grown children still in the household.

Deep River was a McMichael-and-Pegram community. James's daughter Sally would later marry William Franklin McMichael (whose own paternal grandmother was a Pegram — Rhoda Pegram, who had married William Aydlott McMichael Sr.). So the Pegram and McMichael families had been intermarrying in Deep River for two generations by the time Geneva was born in 1891.

James lived to be eighty-four. He died on the 13th of June, 1915, and was buried the following day in Deep River — the year the Lusitania was sunk, the same week the Battle of Gallipoli was at its bloodiest, and the year before the United States would enter the Great War. The News & Observer of Raleigh ran his death notice on the 25th of June.
