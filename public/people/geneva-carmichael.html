Harvey Line — Generation 2

Geneva Carmichael Harvey

fl. 1925–1947

Mother of Ruth and Esther — named in her daughter's 2016 obituary as Geneva Carmichael Harvey. Her parentage and her exact line back to the Highland Carmichael migration remain to be traced.

Partially documented

Geneva Carmichael Harvey (fl. 1925–1947) ⚭ Carl (Carol) Harvey │ ▼ Ruth Garland Harvey Quesenberry (1925–2016) Esther Harvey Scott (1929–2021)

Geneva Carmichael Harvey is, on the present record, a woman known only by her name, her maiden surname, and her two daughters. No Find a Grave entry has surfaced; no death certificate has been pulled; no census household has been confirmed for her. Closing those gaps is the next step in tracing the Carmichael side of the family.

What we do know is that her surname is one of the great Scottish names. Carmichael — from a parish in Lanarkshire, Caer (a fort) plus Michael — is a sept of Clan Douglas, of Clan MacDougall, and of the Stewarts of Appin and of Galloway. The major Carmichael migration to North Carolina came in two great waves in the 1770s: a 1773 sailing from Fort William on the Firth of Lorne carrying 425 Highland emigrants to North Carolina, and the 1775 voyage of the Jupiter of Larne — on which the twenty-one-year-old Archibald Carmichael of Lanarkshire crossed with his wife Mary and infant daughter Katherine, bound for Wilmington.

The Carmichaels of the Carolinas — and there are many of them — are very largely descended from these eighteenth-century Highland Scots. Archibald's documented descendants spread through Cumberland, Richmond (now Scotland), Stokes, and Forsyth Counties. Whether Geneva descends from Archibald specifically, or from one of the other Highland Carmichael families who came in the same migration, has not yet been verified. The geography fits — Geneva's daughter Ruth was born in Guilford County, adjacent to Forsyth and Stokes — but a direct documentary line is the work of the next pass.

What can be said: Geneva passed to her daughter Ruth a Highland Scottish name that had crossed an ocean in a wooden ship before the United States existed. Ruth in turn carried that name into her own life, and forward through her descendants.
