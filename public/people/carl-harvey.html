Harvey Line — Generation 2

Carl (Carol) Harvey

fl. 1925–1947

Father of Ruth (1925) and Esther (1929) — a Colfax, North Carolina farmer whose first name is preserved in two slightly different spellings in his daughters' obituaries. His own grave has not yet been located.

Partially documented

Carl (Carol) Harvey (fl. 1925–1947) ⚭ Geneva Carmichael Harvey │ ▼ Ruth Garland Harvey Quesenberry (1925–2016) Esther Harvey Scott (1929–2021)

The first thing to settle about Lonnie's father-in-law is the simplest question: what was his name? The 2016 Find a Grave biography of his daughter Ruth records him as Carl Harvey. But the 2021 obituary of Ruth's younger sister Esther — preserved on the Pierce-Jefferson Funeral Home website in Kernersville — names him as Carol Harvey. Two daughters, two slightly different spellings. Either is plausible: "Carol" was, in the early twentieth century, an occasional male given name (as it remained in some communities into the 1920s), and the fact that Ruth herself named her own youngest daughter Carol Jeanette is suggestive — it was customary in the Piedmont South to honor a grandfather by giving his name to a granddaughter. On balance, the Esther-obituary spelling "Carol" is the more likely original.

Beyond his name, the publicly accessible record presently says: he was a man of Colfax, in the rolling farm country of western Guilford County, North Carolina. He married Geneva Carmichael. They had at least two daughters, Ruth (born 1925) and Esther (born 1929). Both daughters were raised in the Wesleyan Church — Ruth at Greenwood Hills Wesleyan in High Point, Esther at Shady Grove Wesleyan in Colfax — which suggests the family itself was Wesleyan, an unusual and distinctive denominational identity in the otherwise Methodist and Baptist Piedmont.

He died sometime before January 2016 (when Ruth's obituary refers to him as "the late"). No Find a Grave entry, no North Carolina death record, no 1910/1920/1930/1940 census household for the Carl/Carol Harvey of Colfax has yet been pulled in this research pass. The next reasonable steps: (1) the Shady Grove Wesleyan Church Cemetery in Colfax, where Esther is buried — Carol may be there too. (2) The North Carolina Death Index 1908–2004 at FamilySearch. (3) The Guilford County Register of Deeds. (4) The Heritage Research Center at the Greensboro Public Library, which holds the High Point Enterprise and Greensboro News & Record on microfilm.

What is certain is that he raised two daughters who both lived to old age, both married, both were buried back among the Wesleyan congregations of the Carolina Piedmont — and that one of them named her own daughter for him.
