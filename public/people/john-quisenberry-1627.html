Quesenberry Line — Generation 8

John Quisenberry Sr.

1627–1717

First American-born Quisenberry — planter of Westmoreland County, husband of Anne Pope, by marriage a distant cousin of George Washington.

✓ Documented from public records

John Quisenberry was born in 1627 in colonial Virginia — three years after his father Thomas had stepped off the ship from Kent. He was, on the present record, the first American-born Quesenberry.

Sometime after 1658 he married Anne Pope of Westmoreland County, of the Pope family that had been planters in the Virginia Tidewater since the 1640s. The Anne Pope whom John Quisenberry married was a cousin of another Anne Pope — the Anne Pope who had married Colonel John Washington in 1658. Colonel Washington was the great-grandfather of President George Washington. The two Pope cousins worshiped at the same church and lived as neighbors in Westmoreland County. Through this marriage the Quesenberrys and the Washingtons are distant kin — a connection now verified through the National Park Service's biographical material on Anne Pope Washington and through corroborating Pope-family genealogies on WikiTree.

John was a wealthy planter. He owned large tracts in old Northumberland County (now Westmoreland), Virginia. His will, dated the 23rd of November 1714, was probated three years later, on the 27th of November 1717. He had three sons — John (who died childless), William (1672–1762), and Humphrey (d. before 1727) — and through William and Humphrey the American Quesenberry, Quisenberry, and Christenberry families fan out across the colonies and, eventually, the continent.

He lived to be about ninety. When he was born, Charles I sat on the English throne and Massachusetts was barely a colony. When he died, George I was on the British throne and the American population had crossed half a million.
