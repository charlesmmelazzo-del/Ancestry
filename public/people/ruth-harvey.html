Ruth Garland Harvey Quesenberry

1925–2016

Valedictorian, lifelong cook, devoted mother — born when Calvin Coolidge was president, she lived through the second American century almost entire.

✓ Documented from public records

Ruth Garland Harvey was born on the 27th of June, 1925, in Guilford County, North Carolina — daughter of Carl Harvey and Geneva Carmichael Harvey. The Carmichael name on her mother's side carried the unmistakable echo of the Scottish Highlands: Lanarkshire, the Isle of Lismore, the migration ships of the 1770s that carried the Argyll Scots to Wilmington when North Carolina was still a royal colony.

She grew up in Colfax, a small farming community west of Greensboro that had been called "Dover" before the 1800s and was first settled by families coming south from Pennsylvania and Virginia. There, at Colfax High School, she earned the highest distinction her school could grant: she was named valedictorian of her graduating class — the top student in her year. In a small, rural Piedmont school in the 1940s, this was no small thing.

She was, the obituary remembered, "an excellent self-taught cook" — the highest compliment in a Southern family. She married Lonnie Olin Quesenberry on the 17th of July, 1948, in Greensboro, and together they made a home in High Point. She joined the Greenwood Hills Wesleyan Church on North Main Street, a congregation in the Wesleyan-Methodist tradition that had broken from the larger Methodist body in 1843 over the question of slavery — a denomination that prided itself, even into the twentieth century, on plain living and quiet conviction.

Ruth outlived her husband by fourteen years and her son Phil by one. When she died on the 21st of January, 2016, at Shannon Gray Health Care, she left behind two daughters, six grandchildren, eleven great-grandchildren, and one great-great-grandchild — a small republic of descendants stretching across the Carolinas and beyond.
