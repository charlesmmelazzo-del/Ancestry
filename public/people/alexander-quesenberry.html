Quesenberry Line — Generation 3

Alexander Henry Quesenberry, Jr.

1863–1937

Born the year his father died at Gettysburg's bloody summer — raised by his widowed mother Rebecca Smyth — lived seventy-four years across Reconstruction and the Industrial Age.

Documented

Pvt. Alexander Henry Quesenberry, Sr. (1842–1863) Rebecca Jane Smyth Quesenberry Travis (1844–1920) │ ▼ Alexander Henry Quesenberry (1863–1937) ⚭ Matilda Jane Price Quesenberry │ ▼ Floyd Harmon Quesenberry (1884–1970)

Alexander Henry Quesenberry, Jr. was born in 1863 — the great year of the American Civil War, the year of Gettysburg and Vicksburg. His father, Pvt. Alexander Henry Quesenberry, Sr., was a Confederate Private who died that same year at age twenty-one — making Alexander a posthumous child of the war. He grew up in the household of his widowed teenage mother Rebecca Jane Smyth (1844–1920), in the southwestern Virginia hill country.

He married Matilda Jane Price (1867–1944), daughter of Alexander Stewart Price (1833–1909) and Martha Jane Collins. Together they raised a family that included Floyd Harmon Quesenberry — born in 1885, the bridge to the Tennessee chapter of the family story.

Alexander Jr. died in 1937, age seventy-four. He had lived from the year of Gettysburg to the eve of the Second World War — and had outlived his mother Rebecca by seventeen years.
