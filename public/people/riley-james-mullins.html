Riley James Mullins

1838–1864

Captured Confederate soldier from Claiborne County, Tennessee. Died as a prisoner of war at Rock Island, Illinois — ten months after his cousin Pvt. Alexander Henry Quesenberry Sr. fell at Chancellorsville.

Documented

Military Service Confederate soldier — died as a prisoner of war at the Rock Island, Illinois POW camp, 16 February 1864

Riley James Mullins was born in 1838 in Claiborne County, Tennessee — into the great Mullins clan of the Cumberland Gap mountains. When the Civil War came he served the Confederacy. He was captured at some point and sent north to Rock Island Prison Barracks, a Union POW camp on an island in the Mississippi River between Illinois and Iowa. Rock Island was notorious for harsh conditions and high mortality from smallpox, dysentery, and exposure during the brutal winter of 1863–64. Of the roughly 12,000 Confederates held there, nearly 2,000 died.

Riley James was one of them — dead on the 16th of February, 1864, age 25. He was a cousin of Pvt. Alexander Henry Quesenberry, Sr. (killed at Chancellorsville May 1863), making this family's Confederate cost two young men dead within ten months of each other in the war's middle years.
