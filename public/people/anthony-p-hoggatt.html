Anthony P. Hoggatt

1730–1814

Of the Cedar Creek, Virginia Quaker community. Lived 84 years from George II to James Madison.

Documented

Philip Hoggett (fl. early 1700s) Mary Glendenning Hoggatt (1698–1780) │ ▼ Anthony P. Hoggatt (1730–1814) ⚭ Mary Agnes Stanley Hoggatt

Anthony P. Hoggatt was born on the 8th of February, 1730, at Cedar Creek, in Hanover County, Virginia. Cedar Creek was one of the earliest Quaker meetings established in Virginia — a community of Friends settled by migration from Maryland and Pennsylvania, who held against the established Anglican Church of the colony. He was the son of Mary Hoggatt (1698–1780) and Philip Hoggett.

He married Mary Agnes Hoggatt (1733–1808). The shared surname suggests they were cousins, common in tightly-knit Quaker communities. Anthony lived 84 years through the Seven Years' War, the American Revolution, the founding of the United States, and the early presidencies of George Washington, John Adams, Thomas Jefferson, and James Madison. He died at Cedar Creek on the 7th of August, 1814, just as the British were preparing to burn Washington, D.C. in the War of 1812.
