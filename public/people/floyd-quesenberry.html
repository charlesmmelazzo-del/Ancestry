Quesenberry Line — Generation 2

Floyd Harmon Quesenberry

1884–1970

Born of the old Quesenberry line, raised seven children with Eva Mullins across three states (Tennessee → Norfolk → North Carolina), and lived to eighty-five.

Floyd Harmon Quesenberry was born in 1885, of the old Quesenberry line that had come down from Floyd County, Virginia — the mountain country named for Governor John Floyd, where Quesenberry families had been numerous since the eighteenth century. His father was Alexander H. Quesenberry (1863–1937), and his mother was Matilda Jane Price (1867–1944) — both of the southwestern Virginia uplands.

Sometime before 1920 Floyd married Eva Elizabeth Mullins (1891–1983) and moved south across the state line into Tennessee. Their son Lonnie was born in Kingsport, Sullivan County, Tennessee on 18 March 1920. A second son, Billie R., would follow. By the mid-1930s the family had relocated again — this time east to the Norfolk, Virginia shipyards, where work was plentiful for skilled men in the years before the Second World War.

Floyd lived to be eighty-five, dying in 1970. He had seen his son Lonnie marry, settle in High Point, and raise three children — Phil, Cathey, and Carol — to adulthood. The Quesenberry surname, planted on a wooden ship in 1624 and carried through nine American generations, had reached its third state and its tenth generation in his lifetime.
