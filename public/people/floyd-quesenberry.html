Quesenberry Line — Generation 2

Floyd H. Quesenberry

fl. 1920s

Lonnie's father — named in the 2002 Find a Grave biography of his son. His own dates, parents, and place of birth wait, on the present record, in the courthouse archives of Virginia and North Carolina.

◐ Partial — name documented, other facts await further research

Of Floyd H. Quesenberry, the publicly accessible record presently says only this: he was the father of Lonnie Olin Quesenberry, born in 1920. We have his name and his middle initial, and nothing more. No Find a Grave entry, no death certificate, no census household has yet been located in the public sources searched. The work of placing him precisely in the documented Quesenberry tree remains unfinished.

Two clues nevertheless point in a direction. First, his given name. Floyd almost certainly traces to Floyd County, Virginia — formed from Montgomery County in 1831 and named for Governor John Floyd. To name a son "Floyd" in the Quesenberry country of the late nineteenth century was to plant a flag of identity in a particular patch of the Blue Ridge. Second, his surname: the 1840 federal census found more than seventy percent of all Quesenberrys in the United States living in Virginia, and the great majority of those were within twenty miles of Indian Valley.

It is plausible that Floyd descends from one of the nine Confederate sons of John Quesenberry Sr. and Nancy Hylton — most likely from William Henry Clay Quesenberry (1842–1898) or one of his brothers — but no documentary evidence yet establishes the link. A definitive answer almost certainly waits in the deed books, marriage records, and probate files at the Floyd County, Carroll County, or Davidson County (NC) courthouses, or in the Quesenberry Family Papers at the Virginia Museum of History & Culture in Richmond.

What can be said with confidence is that Floyd H. Quesenberry stood at the hinge of his family's migration — between the Virginia mountainside his Quesenberry kin had farmed for five or six generations, and the Carolina Piedmont where his son Lonnie was born, lived, and died.
