Archibald Carmichael Sr.

1754–1827

Lanarkshire-born, sailed from Larne on the Jupiter in 1775, planted the Carmichael name in the North Carolina red clay.

On the 4th of September, 1775 — his twenty-first birthday — Archibald Carmichael of Lanarkshire, Scotland, stepped aboard the ship Jupiter of Larne in the harbor of Larne, County Antrim, Ulster, with his young wife Mary and his infant daughter Katherine. The ship was bound for Wilmington, North Carolina. The American Revolution was already underway: Lexington and Concord had been fought four months before; the Continental Congress was meeting in Philadelphia.

Archibald was part of the great Highland Scots emigration to the Cape Fear region of North Carolina — a migration that had begun in the 1730s under the leadership of Argyll men like Neil MacNeill, swelled in the 1770s when economic conditions in the Highlands collapsed, and would shape the dialect, the religion, and the surnames of the inland Carolina Piedmont for generations.

Archibald settled first in Caswell County, then in what became Stokes County (today Forsyth County), where he is recorded in the 1800 federal census. He married, after his first wife's passing, Elizabeth Nix of North Carolina, and together they had at least eight children — Elizabeth, Sterling, Jane, Richard, Sarah, Alexander, Joseph, and Archibald Jr.

He died in 1827 and was buried at the Carmichael Cemetery near Stanleyville, in Forsyth County. The exact spot of his grave has been lost to time. His descendants spread through North Carolina and, beginning in 1829, on into Indiana — settling Monroe and Greene Counties.

It is from this Carmichael migration, almost certainly, that Geneva Carmichael — Ruth Garland Harvey's mother — descends. Her exact path back to Archibald (or to one of his cousins or nephews from the same Highland migration) is one of the most rewarding research projects awaiting a future generation of this family.
