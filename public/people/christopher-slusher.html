Quesenberry Line — Generation 5

Christopher Slusher (Schlosser)

1757–1845

Revolutionary War soldier from Lancaster County, Pennsylvania — DAR Patriot Ancestor #A105465 — and the German-American great-grandfather of Eva Jane Slusher.

✓ Documented from public records

⚔ American Revolution — DAR Patriot Ancestor #A105465 (Pennsylvania service)

Christopher Slusher — born Schlosser, the family name still spelled in the original German — came into the world on the 15th of October, 1757, in Lancaster County, Pennsylvania. He was the son of Pennsylvania-Dutch parents whose ancestors had crossed the Atlantic from the Palatinate and settled among the great Pennsylvania-German farming community of the early eighteenth century.

He was eighteen when the American Revolution began, and he served — he is recorded today as a verified DAR Patriot Ancestor, #A105465, with documented Revolutionary War service from Pennsylvania. The exact unit and dates are recorded in the DAR archives in Washington; a SAR application or DAR membership application through his line would surface the specific service record.

After the war, like many Pennsylvania-German families of his generation, he migrated south down the Great Wagon Road into the Virginia and Carolina backcountry. He married Eve Hancock (1761–1838), and together they raised a family in Floyd County, Virginia — the same Blue Ridge country where Christopher's grandsons and great-grandsons would marry into the Quesenberry, Hylton, and Cox families.

He lived to be eighty-eight, dying on the 11th of December, 1845, in Floyd County. His granddaughter Eva Jane Slusher would marry William Henry Clay Quesenberry twenty-two years after his death, joining the Slusher and Quesenberry lines that this site traces forward to Lonnie Olin Quesenberry.
