Harvey Line — Generation 7

Martha E. Newlin Harvey

1721–1806

Born Newlin in colonial Pennsylvania, married Harvey, migrated south to North Carolina. The bridge between two Quaker emigrant lines.

Documented

Nathaniel Newlin (1690–1732) Jane Newlin (1687–?) │ ▼ Martha E. Newlin Harvey (1721–1806) ⚭ Isaac L. Harvey │ ▼ Nathan Heaver Harvey (1759–1837)

Martha E. Newlin was born on the 18th of September, 1721, at Concord, in Chester County, Pennsylvania — into the famous Newlin Quaker family. Her father, Nathaniel Newlin (1690–1732), was the son of Nicholas Newlin, an Irish Quaker who had emigrated from Mountmellick, Queen's County, Ireland to William Penn's new colony in 1683. Her mother was Jane Newlin (b. 1687).

She married Isaac L. Harvey (1718–1802) of the Pennsylvania Harveys, joining the Newlin and Harvey Quaker lines into one. Together they raised Nathan Heaver Harvey (1759–1837), who would lead the family south to North Carolina.

Martha lived an extraordinary 85 years, dying on the 23rd of November, 1806, at Graham, Alamance County, North Carolina — having outlived her husband by four years. Her descendants would farm the Piedmont red clay for five more generations before Geneva McMicheal Harvey (1891–1971), her great-great-great-great-granddaughter, would marry William Carl Harvey and bring the line to Ruth Garland Harvey.
