Harvey Line — Generation 3

Isaac J. Harvey

1842–1912

Widowed twice before he married Ruth's grandmother. Father of seven across three marriages, including a Wesleyan minister.

Documented

Anthony Harvey (fl. early-mid 1800s) Sarah Sally Trueblood Harvey (fl. early-mid 1800s) │ ▼ Isaac J. Harvey (1842–1912) ⚭ Rachel Frances Swift Harvey │ ▼ William Carl Harvey (1887–1966)

Isaac J. Harvey was born on the 29th of March, 1842 in North Carolina — by curious coincidence the same year as Pvt. Alexander Henry Quesenberry, Sr. He was a son of Anthony Harvey and Sarah Sally Trueblood — the Trueblood surname being a famous Quaker name from colonial Virginia and North Carolina, marking the deep Quaker roots of this Harvey line.

Isaac was a man much married. He buried two wives before he married Ruth's grandmother. His first wife was Catherine Reich Harvey (also spelled Rike). After her death he married her sister Henrietta Annetta Reich Harvey — a sororate marriage that was common in 19th-century rural America, where a widower would often marry his deceased wife's unmarried sister to keep the household and the in-laws intact. Henrietta too predeceased him. He then married, finally, Rachel Frances Swift Harvey — daughter of Thomas S. Swift and Lucinda Cox — and with her had William Carl Harvey, born 1887, Ruth's father.

Across the three marriages Isaac fathered at least four known children: Dora C. Harvey, Rev. Addison Melvin Harvey (a Wesleyan minister — continuing the family's religious tradition that ran from Quaker roots through Methodist-Wesleyan into the twentieth century), Sarah E. Harvey, and William Carl Harvey.

Isaac lived to seventy. He died on Friday, the 12th of April, 1912 — the day the RMS Titanic would sink off Newfoundland just three days later. He was buried in his Deep River community the next day, Saturday. The Greensboro Daily News of Saturday April 19, 1912, ran a brief community-news note from a correspondent: 'Isaac Harvey, an aged citizen living near here died Friday morning and was buried here Saturday.' A modest valediction for a man who had outlived two wives, raised four children, and watched the nineteenth century give way to the twentieth.
