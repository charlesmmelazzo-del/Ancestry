Harvey Line — Generation 7

Isaac L. Harvey

1718–1802

Born at Chadds Ford in 1718 — site of the 1777 Battle of Brandywine — and lived 84 years, witnessing the founding of the United States.

Documented

William HARVEY (colonial) (fl. late 1600s – early 1700s) Judith Deuce Osborne Harvey (1683–1770) │ ▼ Isaac L. Harvey (1718–1802) ⚭ Martha E. Newlin Harvey │ ▼ Nathan Heaver Harvey (1759–1837)

Isaac L. Harvey was born on the 21st of September, 1718, at Chadds Ford in Chester County, Pennsylvania. The placename matters: Chadds Ford sits on the Brandywine Creek, on the road from Philadelphia to Wilmington. In September 1777 — when Isaac was 58 — that very ground would become the site of the Battle of Brandywine, the largest battle of the American Revolution measured by combatants engaged. George Washington's Continental Army would face Sir William Howe's British Regulars on Isaac's home soil. Whether he was still living there in 1777, or had already moved south to North Carolina, is uncertain. What is certain is that Chadds Ford was Quaker country — Brandywine Friends Meeting was one of the earliest in the colony.

He married Martha E. Harvey (born Newlin, 1721–1806), of the famous Pennsylvania Newlin Quaker family — daughter of Nathaniel Newlin (1690–1732) and Jane Newlin (b. 1687), the Irish Quaker emigrants who had come to Penn's colony in 1683 from Mountmellick, Ireland. Through this marriage the Harvey and Newlin Quaker lines joined.

They raised at least one son, Nathan Heaver Harvey (1759–1837), who would carry the family south to Orange County, North Carolina with the great Quaker migration down the Great Wagon Road. Isaac himself eventually made the same journey — he died on the 3rd of April, 1802, in Orange County, NC, age 83.
