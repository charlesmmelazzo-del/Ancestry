Question · Priority MEDIUM · related to Quesenberry line

What was the complete service record of each of John Quesenberry Sr.'s nine Confederate sons?

We have the names of all nine — George Washington, Elijah, Thomas Frederick, Archelaus, Nathaniel Floyd, John Jr., William Henry Clay, Amos Ballard, James Montgomery — and partial regimental information (54th VA Infantry, 49th VA Infantry Co. G). What's missing: the complete muster roll, ranks, dates of enlistment and discharge or death, units, battles, and post-war fates. The Quesenberry Family Papers (1827–1913) at the Virginia Museum of History & Culture in Richmond preserve the 1862 muster roll of Co. G, 49th VA Infantry, and family correspondence.

Most likely leads:

  • Virginia Museum of History & Culture, Richmond — Quesenberry Family Papers (research visit required)
  • Compiled Service Records, National Archives (digitized on Fold3)
  • Floyd County and Carroll County local Civil War rosters (Carroll County Chronicles, etc.)
  • WikiTree and Find a Grave for each named son

Question · Priority HIGH · related to Quesenberry line

Who was 'A. Quisenberry' (1842–1863) and what was the cause of his death at age 21?

Mike's Ancestry tree records an ancestor named only by initial — A. Quisenberry, born 1842, died 1863. He was Floyd Quesenberry's grandfather, the father of Alexander H Quesenberry (1863–1937). The math is conspicuous: dead at 21, in the year of Gettysburg, Vicksburg, and Chickamauga. Almost certainly a Confederate soldier. Resolving his full name and military service — and confirming the cause of death — would close one of the most evocative gaps in the family record.

Most likely leads:

  • Floyd County and Carroll County, Virginia Civil War rosters
  • Compiled Service Records (Confederate), National Archives — searchable on Fold3
  • 1860 federal census for the Quesenberry household of Crockett & Nancy J — A. should be listed as a son age ~18
  • 1850 federal census — A. would be ~8 years old
  • Quesenberry Family Papers, Virginia Museum of History & Culture, Richmond
  • Find a Grave search 'A Quisenberry 1863' in Floyd, Carroll, or Pulaski Counties VA

Question · Priority HIGH · related to Quesenberry line

What war did Peter Moore Sr., Pvt. serve in?

Mike's Ancestry tree preserves an ancestor recorded as Peter Moore Sr., Pvt. — the Pvt. in his name marks him as a military veteran. He was the father of Elizabeth Moore (1800–1850), wife of James S. Quesenberry. Given Elizabeth's birth in 1800, Peter likely served in the American Revolution (1775–1783) or the War of 1812. Either would make him a verified Patriot Ancestor — and thus a third DAR/SAR-eligible ancestor for the family (alongside Aaron Quisenberry #A093307 and Christopher Slusher #A105465).

Most likely leads:

  • DAR Patriot Index — search 'Peter Moore'
  • SAR application files, Library of the Sons of the American Revolution, Louisville KY
  • Revolutionary War Pension Files, National Archives (M804 series)
  • War of 1812 Pension Files, National Archives (M313 series)
  • Compiled Service Records — National Archives

Question · Priority LOW · related to Harvey line

What was Elizabeth Deuce's maiden name?

Elizabeth Deuce (wife of Samuel Deuce, mother of Judith Deuce Osborne Harvey) is indexed across multiple trees as 'born NN' — meaning the maiden name was Not Noted in the original genealogical record. She lived in Bilston, Staffordshire, England in the second half of the seventeenth century. Identifying her family would push the documented Harvey ancestry past 9 generations on the maternal-maternal line. Staffordshire parish records (Quaker, Anglican, and dissenting) for Bilston/Wolverhampton ca. 1660–1700 are the most likely source.

Question · Priority LOW · related to Harvey line

What is the full Glendenning family origin in Edinburgh, Scotland?

David George Glendenning and his wife Margaret Hall were the parents of Mary Glendenning Hoggatt (b. 8 Dec 1698, Edinburgh). The Glendenning surname is a Scottish Border family (also seen as Glendinning, Glendening) with documented lineages in Galloway, particularly Drumrash and Parton parishes, dating back to the late medieval period. Identifying which Glendenning line in Edinburgh produced David George — and what carried his family into the early eighteenth-century emigration to colonial America — would push the documented Scottish ancestry back further. The Hoggatt-Glendenning emigration to Pennsylvania ca. 1715–1720 was likely tied to the Quaker migration to William Penn's colony.

Most likely leads:

  • National Records of Scotland, Edinburgh — parish registers for Edinburgh parishes ca. 1695–1720
  • Scottish Genealogy Society library, Edinburgh
  • Quaker Yearly Meeting records, Scotland — early Friends
  • Geni World Family Tree continued research on the Glendenning family of Edinburgh
  • Records of departures from Scottish ports to Pennsylvania, 1715–1725

Question · Priority HIGH · related to Quesenberry line

Who were Floyd H. Quesenberry's parents?

Floyd H. Quesenberry is named in Lonnie's 2002 Find a Grave biography, but no birth date, death date, parents, or census household has yet been located in publicly accessible records. The given name Floyd almost certainly traces to Floyd County, Virginia — formed 1831, named for Governor John Floyd. Surname concentration in the 1840 census placed >70% of all U.S. Quesenberrys in Virginia, the great majority within twenty miles of Indian Valley. It is plausible that Floyd descends from one of the nine Confederate sons of John Quesenberry Sr. and Nancy Hylton — most likely from William Henry Clay Quesenberry's surviving children — but no documentary evidence yet establishes the link.

Most likely leads:

  • Floyd County, VA Courthouse — deed books, marriage records, probate files
  • Carroll County, VA Courthouse — same
  • Davidson County, NC Courthouse — Lonnie was buried there; Floyd may have died there
  • North Carolina marriage records 1900–1930 (FamilySearch)
  • 1900, 1910, 1920, 1930, 1940 federal censuses for any Floyd Quesenberry in VA or NC
  • Quesenberry Family Papers (1827–1913), Virginia Museum of History & Culture, Richmond

Question · Priority HIGH · related to Quesenberry line

What was Eva Quesenberry's maiden name?

Eva is named only as 'Eva Quesenberry' in Lonnie's 2002 Find a Grave biography — under her married surname. In heavily intermarried Appalachian communities it was common for first cousins to marry, so it is possible Eva was born a Quesenberry herself. Equally possible the indexer simply did not record her birth name. The persistence of Eva as a Quesenberry family name (Eva Jane Slusher, Eve Hancock) makes either reading plausible.

Most likely leads:

  • Marriage record of Floyd H. Quesenberry (likely Floyd Co. VA or Davidson Co. NC, 1910s–early 1920s)
  • Lonnie's birth certificate (NC Vital Records, 1920) would name his mother
  • 1920, 1930 census — Eva should appear in the household with her birthplace and parents' birthplaces

Question · Priority MEDIUM · related to Quesenberry line

Where is Lonnie Olin Quesenberry's WWII draft card and (any) service record?

Lonnie was 21 in 1941 and like virtually every American man born in 1920 was required to register for the WWII draft. A draft card almost certainly exists in the National Archives WWII Draft Registration Cards collection (FamilySearch Collection 1968530, also indexed on Ancestry.com and Fold3). The card itself records date of registration, residence, employer, height, weight, complexion, and nearest relative. Whether he served in the war (vs. only registered) is a separate question.

Most likely leads:

  • FamilySearch Collection 1968530 — search 'Lonnie Olin Quesenberry' in NC
  • Ancestry.com WWII Draft Cards (same collection, indexed)
  • Fold3.com — sometimes has clearer scans
  • National Personnel Records Center, St. Louis — for any service record (separate from draft card)

Question · Priority MEDIUM · related to Mcmichael line

RESOLVED: Geneva's mother is Sally Pegram (daughter of James Fletcher Pegram)

Resolved 18 May 2026 via MyHeritage record-match deep dive. James Fletcher Pegram's 10 record matches (FamilySearch Family Tree, NC marriages, 1900 & 1910 federal censuses) all consistently name his children, including daughter Sally Pegram who later took the surname McMichael. Sally Pegram is Geneva's mother. The 'Esther Jane McMichael' surname on the Find a Grave index for Geneva's burial appears to have been an indexer conflation with Geneva's own middle name Esther. The Pegram-McMichael family lived in Deep River Township, Guilford County, NC across multiple generations. Note that the McMichael-Pegram intermarriage runs three generations deep: Jesse C. McMichael's MOTHER was also a Pegram (Rhoda E. Pegram McMichael), making Geneva descended from Pegrams via two distinct lines.

Most likely leads:

  • Closed — see new entries for James Fletcher Pegram, Sally Pegram, Jesse C. McMichael, and Rhoda E. Pegram in the people directory
