Bromley, Kent, England

📍 51.404, 0.014

Birthplace of Thomas Questenbury, 1608. The first link in the American Quesenberry chain.

A market town in northwest Kent, on the southeastern fringe of London. In 1608 it was a parish of farms and orchards, home to the Questenbury family — yeomen of the kind who, when colonial labor was needed in the new Virginia plantations, sent their younger sons across the Atlantic. From Bromley to Jamestown was the first leg of the journey that would, four hundred years later, end in High Point, North Carolina.

Family connections: Thomas Questenbury

Kingsport, Sullivan County, Tennessee

📍 36.548, -82.562

Birthplace of Lonnie Olin Quesenberry, 18 March 1920. A planned industrial town in the far northeast corner of Tennessee.

Kingsport sits in the far northeast corner of Tennessee, where the Holston River runs out of southwestern Virginia and the Cumberland Gap is one ridge over. The town was incorporated only three years before Lonnie's birth — in 1917 — as a planned industrial community built around a Mead pulp-and-paper mill. By 1920 it was a fast-growing center of work for southern Appalachian families like the Quesenberrys, who had drifted south and west from the old Floyd County, Virginia mountains. From Kingsport the Quesenberrys would migrate east to the Norfolk shipyards by the mid-1930s.

Norfolk, Virginia (Western Branch / Bowers Hill)

📍 36.851, -76.286

Where the Quesenberry family lived through the Great Depression and the buildup to WWII — five miles from the great Naval Operating Base.

By 1935 Floyd Harmon Quesenberry, Eva, and their sons Lonnie and Billie had left Tennessee for Western Branch and Bowers Hill — neighborhoods on the western edge of Norfolk, Virginia, abutting the great Naval Operating Base. The federal census of 1940 records the household here. Lonnie filed his WWII Draft Registration Card from Bowers Hill in 1941. The Norfolk shipyards were the engine of America's Atlantic war effort; the family's presence here on the eve of Pearl Harbor placed them at the center of the largest industrial mobilization in American history.

Family connections: Lonnie Olin Quesenberry · Floyd Harmon Quesenberry · Eva Elizabeth Mullins Quesenberry · Billie Ray Quesenberry

Deep River, Guilford County, North Carolina

📍 36.081, -79.789

The McMichael-Harvey farming community where Geneva McMichael grew up and where she and William Carl Harvey raised Ruth and Esther.

Deep River was, for generations, a rural farming district in southwestern Guilford County, North Carolina — adjacent to (and in some periods overlapping with) Colfax. The 1900 federal census found Geneva McMichael, age 8, living in the Deep River household of her father William Franklin McMichael. After her 1919 marriage to William Carl Harvey, the 1930 and 1950 censuses again record the Harvey household at Deep River. Three generations of the family worked the same red Piedmont clay here.

Family connections: Geneva Esther McMicheal Harvey · William Carl Harvey · Ruth Garland Harvey Quesenberry · Esther Harvey Scott · William Franklin McMichael

Floyd County, Virginia

📍 36.931, -80.378

The Quesenberry mountain country. Indian Valley, Greasy Creek, and the long ridges of the Blue Ridge.

Created in 1831 from Montgomery County, Floyd County is the high country of southwestern Virginia — a country of sheep pastures, hardwood ridges, and creek hollows where the Quesenberry, Slusher, Hylton, and Cox families intermarried for five generations. From Floyd, in the 1860s, John Quesenberry Sr. and Nancy Hylton sent nine sons into the Confederate Army. From Floyd, in the early twentieth century, the Quesenberrys began the migration south into the Carolina Piedmont — a migration that ended with Lonnie Olin Quesenberry's birth in North Carolina in 1920.

Family connections: William Henry Clay Quesenberry · Eva Jane Slusher Quesenberry · John Quesenberry Sr. · George Quesenberry · Christopher Slusher (Schlosser)

Colfax, Guilford County, North Carolina

📍 36.105, -79.986

The Harvey-McMichael farming community west of Greensboro. Birthplace of Ruth and Esther Harvey.

A rural hamlet in western Guilford County, named for Schuyler Colfax (Vice President under Grant) when the railroad came through after the Civil War. Before that it was called Dover. The Harvey and McMichael families farmed here for generations, worshiped at Shady Grove Wesleyan Church, and sent their daughters to Colfax High School — where Ruth Garland Harvey was named valedictorian in the 1940s. Geneva Esther McMicheal Harvey was buried at Colfax in 1971.

Family connections: Ruth Garland Harvey Quesenberry · Esther Harvey Scott · William Carl Harvey · Geneva Esther McMicheal Harvey

High Point, Guilford County, North Carolina

📍 36.081, -79.789

The furniture-making capital of the South — and the home Lonnie & Ruth made together for fifty-four years.

By the time Lonnie and Ruth married in 1948, High Point was the established center of American furniture manufacturing — the great showrooms drawing buyers from across the country twice a year. They settled here, raised three children, joined Greenwood Hills Wesleyan Church on North Main Street, and lived here the rest of their lives. Both are buried at Holly Hill Memorial Park in adjacent Thomasville.

Family connections: Lonnie Olin Quesenberry · Ruth Garland Harvey Quesenberry
